A thoughtful guide to buying handmade

Explore traditional handmade crafts, learn how they are made, and find reliable places to buy them at fair prices. This guide uses sample listings to show what to ask, compare and check before taking a souvenir home.

Good buying starts with a good question: ask who made the item, where the material came from and how the technique was passed on.

Explore Craft Categories

These broad categories help visitors recognise common handmade techniques and understand where an item may have come from.

Handloom Textiles

Woven cotton, wool and silk pieces made on a hand-operated loom.

Traditional technique
Warping, shuttle weaving and hand-finishing.
Region or origin
Sample entry: hill and plains weaving communities.
Typical examples
Scarves, table runners, shawls and small bags.

Ceramic Pottery

Useful and decorative clay objects shaped, dried and fired in a kiln.

Traditional technique
Wheel throwing or hand building with slip decoration.
Region or origin
Sample entry: village pottery clusters near river clay deposits.
Typical examples
Cups, oil lamps, bowls and small painted figurines.

Wood Carvings

Small objects shaped from locally available wood by carving and sanding.

Traditional technique
Relief carving, chiselling and natural oil finishing.
Region or origin
Sample entry: forest-edge craft communities.
Typical examples
Boxes, birds, utensils and wall panels.

Metal Craft

Decorative or household pieces made by shaping, casting or engraving metal.

Traditional technique
Lost-wax casting, hammering or hand engraving.
Region or origin
Sample entry: established metalworking neighbourhoods.
Typical examples
Small bells, trays, lamps and animal figures.

Leather Goods

Practical accessories cut, stitched and finished by hand.

Traditional technique
Pattern cutting, saddle stitching and edge burnishing.
Region or origin
Sample entry: family-run leather workshops in old market lanes.
Typical examples
Wallets, belts, sandals and notebook covers.

Souvenir Fair Price Guide

These are benchmark ranges intended to help visitors identify unusually high tourist prices. They are not official or guaranteed prices and should be adjusted for the local market.

Handloom textile
Typical item: Cotton scarf or runner
Approximate fair-price range: 800–2,500 local currency units
What affects price: Fibre, length, weave density, dye and maker time. Advice: Compare two or three similar pieces and ask whether the item is handwoven.
Small ceramic item
Typical item: Cup, bowl or lamp
Approximate fair-price range: 250–900 local currency units
What affects price: Firing, glaze, detail and breakage-safe packing. Advice: Ask whether the glaze is food-safe when relevant.
Wooden carving
Typical item: Small bird, box or utensil
Approximate fair-price range: 500–2,000 local currency units
What affects price: Wood type, size, carving detail and finish. Advice: Ask about legal sourcing of the wood.
Metal craft item
Typical item: Bell, tray or small lamp
Approximate fair-price range: 600–3,000 local currency units
What affects price: Metal weight, casting, engraving and polishing. Advice: Compare weight and workmanship, not only the shine.
Leather handicraft
Typical item: Wallet, belt or notebook cover
Approximate fair-price range: 700–2,800 local currency units
What affects price: Leather grade, lining, stitching and hardware. Advice: Inspect the edges and ask how the leather was treated.

Prices are indicative benchmarks and may vary depending on materials, craftsmanship, size and location.

Visitor Tips

  • Ask about the craft's origin.
  • Check whether a GI claim is supported.
  • Compare prices between sellers.
  • Ask whether the item is handmade or factory-produced.
  • Request a receipt when appropriate.
